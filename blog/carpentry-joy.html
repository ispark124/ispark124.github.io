carpentry & joy

Isaiah Park

Do you think Jesus liked being a carpenter? Like genuinely enjoyed the work that He was doing? Do you think He was excited waking up to go to work and build a table or something. It's kind of crazy to think about. The Son of God, God incarnate, coming down to Earth to do some woodworking projects. I was looking into it and boys at the time started their Fathers' trade at the age of 12, which means 18 years of carpentry before Jesus even started His ministry. Which also means He spent six times as much time being a carpenter than preaching the gospel. So I went back and checked–there's not even a single woodworking parable He tells from His time in ministry. Genuinely what was it all for?

I'm about a month into my job now and honestly the whole working every single day, living the 9 to 5 schedule, and raising shareholder value bit has me a little frustrated. Like really what is it all for? Admittedly, part of it is the naivety of being post-grad thinking the rest of my life is just going to be perfect now that I'm independent and everything that comes with it. But I do think there's a lot I'm wrestling with as it comes to purpose, meaning, and my work (shocker, I know). These days I find myself thinking often about Jesus' time on Earth. Surely He was frustrated too. Surely when Jesus was 22, 10 years into doing carpentry, He woke up thinking, "I was made for more than this." He had every right to–that's literally God Himself fixing His neighbor's chair (on second thought, that sounds exactly like God, doesn't it?) Regardless, I'm wishing He was more frustrated. It gives me an excuse to be discontent about what I'm doing. Surely He did the same.

But also taking that thought further, in what world does the Son of God, God incarnate, come down to Earth to suffer and die? Building tables doesn't seem that glorious, but in comparison to being beaten, mocked, and scorned, it's a wonderful trade. And yet, He went to the cross for the joy set before Him. What a horribly beautiful purpose. As I reflect on that verse, "the joy set before Him," I'm brought to a couple different thoughts:

  1. That joy was us. That's what I have heard time and time again growing up in church. Jesus died for us. He loves us so much that He would endure the suffering and injustice of the cross. This is the gospel.
  2. After some more reflection, that joy was also the Father. Jesus understood more than us all, "For I have come down from heaven, not to do my own will but the will of him who sent me." The will of the Father.This is the gospel.

What a horribly beautiful purpose.

And yet, what a joy to do the Father's will.

The call of God is sweet because it is always unto Himself. To be obedient to God is a joy in and of itself, because we long to do the will of the Father. And if you're still iffy on if God's will is better than whatever you've got planned, welcome to the club. As a long time member of the club, I can say that the journey does get better. Not because there is less suffering (look at Jesus' journey) but because there is great purpose and joy in it (look at Jesus' journey).

I don't think work's gonna change or even that I'm suddenly going to find great joy in raising shareholder value (yet). But I look to Jesus, who spent nearly two decades of His life on Earth being a carpenter. Humble, lowly, and satisfied doing the Father's will. So I am looking for the Father's will. And that is enough.

He is enough.
