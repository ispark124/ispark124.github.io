It's that season for the youngins again. These past couple weeks I've been helping my little brother with his college essay and gosh did that bring me back. The whole process of unique, authentic self discovery wrapped neatly for the gaze of a random college admissions officer. Those were the days (I also see the irony in the fact that I do this for fun now for the gaze of whoever happens to follow along with the blog. What can I say, love of the game). 

Throughout these past couple weeks of helping my brother, I found myself rereading my old college essay. It was actually pretty entertaining to see how my voice developed throughout high school and how it showed up in my essay–by senior year I'd had my blog and been writing for a couple years. Whenever people ask me if I've changed through the years I always answer with "No, but I have grown into myself," and reading this essay again really is the proof of that statement. It's definitely cringey at times (surely, as all college essays are), but I do see my present self in the the ways I viewed people, the **lessons** I'd learned, and maybe even my corny-ness. 

Ok, but why am I writing about my college essay? "Bro, you definitely peaked in high school." No, stop, there's a real point to this I promise. Bear with me as I explain my essay and where I'm at now (I promise I didn't peak).

The premise of my essay was how I raced the four **laps** of the mile. Each **lap** had a **lesson** attached to it and I tied each to vignette of my life. **Lap** one is getting a good start–setting myself up well to not get boxed in later. **Lap** two is settling into my pace while not slowing down. The third **lap** is always the hardest part, and I have to push through pain. Finally, give the last **lap** everything I have. No matter what, push to the end. Cute and corny, right? Yeah, I thought so too. 

I was on a long run this morning and ended up on a track. As I was running a mile there to break up the monotony of the road, I thought of each of these **lessons** as I was running my **laps**. And naturally, I start to reflect on my life and get unnecessarily deep into my thoughts as I tend to do on my runs. Previously, I've applied these "**lap lessons**" to a shorter time frame of my life, whether it was just my time in high school or even subconsciously throughout college. But today, I zoomed out to the scope of my entire life–yeah, we're getting real.

Ok stay with me: if I break up my whole life into quarters, roughly 20-25 years, I'm rounding out my first quarter now and heading into my second. I don't think I can every properly sum up my entire life up to this point in a sentence, but it really has been such a joy. It's felt like getting a good start, setting a solid foundation, and positioning myself well. In my few short months of being post-grad, it has taken a turn from the rhythms (on a macro scale) of my entire life. If you've been around me these past few months, I've often talked about my struggles with the mundanity of working, adjusting to the new schedule, and just wrestling with my purpose in all of it. So, in keeping with the **lessons** of my college essay, I'm right on schedule. 

I can't lie, life has definitely been hard moving to a new place and restarting my life. Don't get me wrong, there have been so many sweet moments and budding friendships that make me super excited for the future, but change is also change. For a while, I've been frustrated because life doesn't look like it used to, but that's really what's supposed to happen isn't it. It's the end of the first quarter and onto the next. I'm settling into my pace and I can't slow up. The thing is, I don't want to. If life so far has been so full and sweet and beautiful (here's that corny again), I don't want to miss it even if it looks different. Especially if it looks different, how much more do I have to learn and grow and change in the midst of a new chapter. I know I've been talking and writing about this for a while, but I think it's finally starting to sink in. I'm in a new **lap** of life.

That was a lot of yapping just to say I'm really starting to embody and accept that life looks different than it did before. Nothing really profound, but I did it. Thank you for bearing with me.

I want to start writing more again because I feel like I've had both a lack of written introspection these days and lack of creative outlets. So apologies for not having a super polished post this time around–I am working on another longer thought piece rather than just reflections on my life–but hopefully this just means I'll be writing more frequently. If anything, it's more of my raw voice. 

Sike, I'm just writing more to relive my glory days of when I peaked in high school :P
